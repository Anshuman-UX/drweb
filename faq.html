CHAPTER 08 TECHNICAL INQUIRIES & CLARIFICATIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Detailed technical answers addressing acoustic fluid mechanics, solid-state electrochemistry, regulatory status, and operational parameters.

Standard quadcopters generate high-pitched 2,000–5,000 Hz acoustic whine caused by tip vortex cavitation—violent air turbulence breaking across open blade tips. Garud’s WhisperDrive™ propellers feature closed-loop toroidal geometry molded from continuous carbon filaments. Because the blade loops back into itself without an open edge, tip vortices are physically eliminated. The acoustic energy floor drops to 48.5 dBA at 15 meters, and the remaining sound shifts below 350 Hz, blending harmlessly into natural ambient rustle.

Garud achieves this through dual innovations:

  1. Solid-State Lithium-Sulfur (Li-S) Chemistry: Delivering 410 Wh/kg specific energy density, almost double the capacity of standard 220 Wh/kg lithium-polymer packs.
  2. Autoclaved Carbon Monocoque: Curing the fuselage as a single continuous hollow structure eliminates over 40 redundant steel screws, brackets, and heavy plastic shells, bringing total airframe dry weight to just 155.8 grams before battery insertion.

Because Garud weighs precisely 249 grams at takeoff (TOW), it falls beneath the critical 250-gram regulatory threshold recognized by the FAA (United States), EASA (European Union), Transport Canada, and DGCA (India). In most jurisdictions, it is exempt from mandatory transponder beacons and restrictive pilot licensing prerequisites for standard open-category flights.

Unlike stereo cameras that depend on ambient daylight and visual edge contrast, OmniSight™ emits active 905nm infrared laser pulses that travel through atmospheric dust and fog. By calculating the round-trip speed of light down to picoseconds for 120,000 points per second, Garud constructs an autonomous 3D volumetric barrier, rendering unlit subway tunnels, mine shafts, and night forests navigable without external spotlights.

If RF signal is lost for more than 1.8 seconds, Garud’s triple-redundant flight computer automatically engages Return-to-Perch protocol. It retraces its exact outbound 3D laser-mapped trajectory to escape localized RF shadows, climbs to a pre-cleared altitude, and navigates back to base using dual-band RTK satellite geodetics, touching down within 1.5 cm of its charging pad.

Standard liquid-electrolyte batteries suffer catastrophic voltage freeze in sub-zero air and risk thermal runaway in desert heat. Garud’s solid ceramic electrolyte eliminates flammable liquid solvents. Furthermore, pyrolytic graphene heat conduits route motor and NPU heat into the battery enclosure in alpine cold, while dissipating excess heat into the carbon monocoque skin during desert flights.

Electronic image stabilization (EIS) crops into the sensor’s pixel grid and applies algorithmic warping, which softens fine details and introduces jelly-like motion artifacts in dim light. Garud’s 3-axis gimbal uses direct-drive brushless motors updating at 2,000 Hz to counteract physical movements mechanically. The entire 45.4-megapixel full-frame sensor remains pristine and uncropped, delivering true edge-to-edge cinema fidelity.

When landing on a moving vessel, Garud receives high-frequency RTK telemetry from the ship’s master perch antenna. High-speed 300 Hz downward infrared cameras lock onto the perch’s kinetic fiducial pattern, dynamically matching the vessel’s forward velocity, pitch, and roll in real time to touch down safely even in rolling Force 5 seas.
